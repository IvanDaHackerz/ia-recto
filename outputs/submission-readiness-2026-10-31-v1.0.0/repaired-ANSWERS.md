## RD31_004

A private hardware store wants the whole missing-cash amount taken from whichever cashier was on duty, without checking who caused the shortage. What conditions apply to a wage deduction?

A wage deduction for missing cash is only allowed if the employer is in a business where such deductions are a recognized practice and the employee is clearly shown to be responsible, is given a reasonable chance to explain why the deduction should not be made, the amount is fair, does not exceed the actual loss, and is not more than 20 % of the employee’s weekly wages. Moreover, it cannot be a withholding without the worker’s consent (Art 116) nor a deduction made merely to benefit the employer or secure continued employment (Art 117).

## RD31_008

A private packaging plant in Pampanga gives a short lunch period and says continuous operation justifies it. What conditions govern a shorter meal break and whether it counts as working time?

Every employer must give employees at least one hour off for regular meals. However, a shorter meal break of not less than twenty minutes is allowed if the employer meets any of these conditions: the work is non‑manual or not physically strenuous; the establishment operates at least sixteen hours a day; there is an actual or impending emergency or urgent work on machinery to avoid serious loss; or the work is needed to prevent serious loss of perishable goods. When a shorter break is given under any of those cases, it is counted as compensable working time. Additionally, coffee or rest breaks lasting from five to twenty minutes are also considered compensable working time.

## RD31_033

An appliance seller in Cabanatuan gave an express warranty but its written card does not say who will honor it, what is covered, or how long it lasts. What warranty information does the law require?

When a seller or manufacturer gives an express warranty, the law requires the warranty to be written in clear, easily understood language and to include: the identity of the warrantor; the identity of the party to whom the warranty is extended; a description of the products or parts covered; a statement of what the warrantor will do if a defect, malfunction or non‑conformity occurs and who will pay for it; a description of what the consumer must do to claim the warranty; and the period within which the warrantor must act after notice of the problem.
