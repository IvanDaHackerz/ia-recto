**Cognitive load and emotional journey**

Four checklist weaknesses appeared across the reviewed flows: chunking, phone hierarchy, number of visible choices, and remembering citation context. Five navigation links and ten Library results are manageable on desktop, but their stacked phone presentation creates unnecessary travel. The generic Library transition also asks users to remember the source they were inspecting.

Arrival feels calm and credible. A scripted example produces a confident answer, but source inspection reveals only fragments, and a freely worded question suddenly exposes the prototype boundary. Early disclosure would prevent that reversal in trust.

**Persona red flags**

- First-time users may take "verified answers" and "Your past conversations" literally without encountering the prototype notice.
- People relying on assistive technology encounter the unnamed Settings switch and no explicit announcement of a new response. Screen-reader behavior itself was not tested.
- Phone users must scroll substantially before asking, then scroll again to find the selected Library document.

**Minor observations**

- An empty Library search shows both "No documents match" and "Select a document." Add a reset action and a useful recovery prompt.
- Desktop answer paragraphs have broad line lengths; clearer answer/evidence reading zones would improve inspection.
- The small sidebar "Text size" caption calculates to approximately 4.425:1 contrast from the declared tokens, narrowly below the 4.5:1 small-text target. This was a token-based calculation, not a comprehensive contrast audit.
- JavaScript smooth scrolling needs to honor reduced-motion preferences.
- Repeated uppercase micro-headings and middle-dot metadata diverge from the prior design guide; these are lower priority than trust and task flow.
